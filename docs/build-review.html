Custom Builder Review

What I'd fix, in the order I'd fix it, based on reading the code rather than looking at the screens.

Hot Cup Factory — custom quote builder
20 Aug 2026
12 findings · 3 blocking
The short version

The design holds. The implementation has three real holes.

The interaction model is sound and I'd ship it — the two-minute thesis survives contact with real photography, the quote vocabulary is consistent everywhere, and the layout is built on your actual theme tokens rather than approximations of them.

But it is a prototype wearing production clothes, and one thing in it is actively untrue. Below, ordered by what would embarrass you soonest.

Read the severities as calendar time, not as judgement. Blocking means don't show it to a customer or a stakeholder until it's fixed. Before launch means it must be right when this becomes a real page. Polish means it will bother you eventually.

Findings

What I'd fix

01

The header makes a promise the code doesn't keep

Blocking

The header reads "Your progress saves automatically." It doesn't. The only thing in local storage is the product photos and the alignment toggle — the quote itself lives in a JavaScript variable and dies the moment the tab closes or the page reloads.

This isn't a missing nicety. It was a decided design point: the whole argument for asking email at screen four instead of screen one was that the list survives without it. Without persistence, the late-email decision loses its safety net and someone who wanders off loses everything.

Fix Persist state.quote and state.answers to local storage on every mutation, restore on load, and clear on successful submit. Roughly twenty lines — the photo persistence already there is the template. Until it exists, delete the header sentence rather than leave it lying.
02

Browser Back destroys the whole session

Blocking

All five screens are one document with hidden toggled by JavaScript. There is no history integration at all — no pushState, no popstate, no hash. So Back doesn't go from Contact to Details; it leaves the builder entirely and takes the quote with it.

On mobile this is worse than it sounds. Back is a system gesture, not a button people choose deliberately, and a multi-screen flow that punishes it feels broken rather than opinionated.

Fix Push a history entry per screen and restore from popstate. With finding 01 done, restoring state is trivial. Also fixes deep-linking, which you'll want anyway when the collection page CTA needs to open the builder on a specific product.
03

Focus is dropped on every screen change

Blocking

Changing screens hides one <section> and shows another, but nothing moves focus. The only .focus() calls in the file are on a form field and a textarea. So after clicking Continue, the browser's focus stays on a button that no longer exists on screen.

For a keyboard user the next Tab starts from nowhere. For a screen reader user nothing is announced at all — the page appears not to have responded.

Fix On screen change, move focus to the new screen's <h1> with tabindex="-1". Three lines, and it fixes the announcement problem at the same time.
04

The tabs have half an ARIA implementation

Before launch

The category row declares role="tablist" and role="tab", but there is no aria-controls, no role="tabpanel" on what they control, and no arrow-key handling anywhere in the file.

Half-implemented ARIA is worse than none. Declaring the role promises a screen reader that arrow keys will work and that each tab owns a panel; neither is true, so assistive tech describes a control that behaves differently from how it just announced itself.

Fix Either complete it — aria-controls, a real tabpanel, arrow-key roving tabindex — or drop the roles and let them be what they actually are: buttons that filter a grid. The second is honest, cheaper, and I'd take it.
05

Form errors are a red border and nothing else

Before launch

Submitting the contact screen with an empty required field focuses it and sets borderColor to orange. No message, no aria-invalid, no summary. A colourblind user gets no signal whatsoever, and a screen reader user gets silence.

Fix Add a per-field message tied by aria-describedby, set aria-invalid, and use the native required attribute so the browser helps rather than being bypassed by novalidate.
06

The disabled Continue never says why

Before launch

On Details, Continue stays disabled until the in-hands date is answered. Correct behaviour, invisible reason. A disabled control with no explanation is one of the most reliable ways to strand someone — they can see the way forward and can't tell what's blocking it.

Fix Keep the button enabled and validate on click, scrolling to the unanswered required question. Failing that, put a line beside it: "Answer the first question to continue."
07

Talk to us is still an alert box

Before launch

Two window.alert() stubs remain — the Talk to us route and the sample box handoff. The second is a genuine placeholder. The first is a decided design element from the flow map: the second door, the escape hatch, and the thing that catches every customer the builder doesn't suit.

Fix Build it as a real route: phone, booking link, short note field — and critically, carry the customer's position in the builder with it, so a rep sees what they were looking at when they gave up. That context was the entire argument for having an escape hatch rather than a dead end.
08

Nothing is announced when the quote changes

Before launch

There are no aria-live regions in the file. Adding a product, removing a line, and the totals changing are all silent to a screen reader — the most important feedback in the interface has no non-visual channel.

Fix One polite live region on the quote rail totals. "12 oz Single Wall added. 2 products, 8,000 units."
09

Every state change re-parses 138KB of images

Polish

renderTiles() rebuilds the whole grid via innerHTML, and there are eleven call sites — tab change, product select, add, remove. Each rebuild re-creates six <img> elements whose src is an inline base64 data URI, so the browser re-decodes them rather than reusing anything.

At six products it's survivable. It won't be if the custom range grows, and it's the kind of thing that reads as sluggishness on a mid-range phone without ever showing up as a bug.

Fix Build tiles once and toggle aria-pressed and the badge on state change instead of re-rendering. When this moves to Shopify the data URIs become real image URLs anyway, which solves the decode cost but not the churn.
10

Ten instances of 9px type

Polish

The spec labels, chip captions and category headers all sit at 9px, several at rgba(0,0,0,0.55) on white. That combination is below what most accessibility guidance treats as a floor, and it's the text carrying your minimums and lead times — the two facts that decide whether a product is even viable.

Fix Lift to 10–11px and darken to around 0.65 opacity. The house spec-grid pattern does use 9px labels, so this is a deliberate divergence from the theme rather than a correction to it — worth making consciously.
11

Six lifestyle photos, six different environments

Polish

Kitchen counter, beige studio sweep, café interior, blue sky, grey concrete, white countertop. Individually they're good shots. In a row of six tiles they read as six unrelated pictures rather than one product range.

Fix Either crop tighter so the product fills more of the frame and the background stops competing, or shoot the tile set on one consistent ground and keep the lifestyle shots for the configurator, where there's room for them to work.
12

Nothing is instrumented

Polish

There are no analytics events anywhere. The flow map argued for tracking the split between the two doors, which tabs get opened without action, and where people abandon — none of that exists yet, and it's the evidence you'd need to know whether any of this worked.

Fix Instrument before launch, not after: door choice, product opened, add to quote, screen reached, abandon point, submit. Cheap to add now, impossible to backfill.
Integration

Three seams to design before wiring

All three are hardcoded, all three are meant to be. But each has a decision inside it worth making deliberately rather than discovering during implementation.

  • →Catalog from ShopifyThe builder derives its categories from a group field on each product, so nothing drifts out of sync. The open question is where minimum, case size and lead time live — they aren't standard Shopify fields, so they need metafields, and that schema should be designed before anything reads it.
  • →Submission to HubSpotThe quote is structured data — line items with product, size and an array of quantities. Squeezing that into a flat form submission loses exactly the structure that justified the project. Worth landing it as a properly typed record a rep can sort and filter.
  • →Images from productsPhotos are currently inline base64 in the page source. In production they should be Shopify product images, which removes the page weight and means marketing can swap a photo without touching code.
Leave alone

What's working, and why I'd not touch it

  • ✓The no-typing thesis survivedEvery decision in the flow is still a tap on something visual until the four contact fields. That held through six rounds of changes, which is the real test of a design principle.
  • ✓Quote vocabulary is consistent everywhereNo cart, no checkout, no order, no total. The reassurance line sits under every continue button. That discipline is easy to lose the moment someone else edits a string, so it's worth writing down.
  • ✓One image function, five surfacesTiles, configurator, quote rail, review and the setup panel all route through shot(). Swapping drawings for photography touched one function rather than four renderers, and swapping to Shopify URLs will do the same.
  • ✓Theme tokens, not approximations4px space unit, 10px card radius, Canopy easing, the house spec-grid pattern. When this becomes a section it should drop into the theme without a visual reconciliation pass.
  • ✓Comparing quantitiesThe one feature that came from you rather than the brief, and the one most likely to save a round trip per enquiry. The "or" phrasing and the comparing tag are doing real work — they're what stops a rep reading two quantities as one big order.
Order of work

If I had a day

Findings 01 through 03 are half a day together and they share a foundation — persistence makes history restoration trivial, and history restoration makes focus management obvious. Do them as one piece rather than three tickets.

Then 07, the Talk to us route, because it's the only remaining stub that represents a decision rather than a placeholder, and because a builder without its escape hatch is a narrower product than the one we designed.

Everything else can wait for the real build — but instrument it (12) before launch, not after. It's the only item on this list that gets harder rather than easier with time.
